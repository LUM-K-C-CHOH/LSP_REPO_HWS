My perspective on using AI in programming definitely changed over the semester. I came into the course seeing AI mostly 
as something that could help generate answers quickly, but over time I started using tools like ChatGPT and Claude more 
as partners for learning, debugging, and thinking through problems. They were genuinely helpful and made things like 
testing and error identification much easier for me. At the same time, I also realized there is a downside when you rely 
on it too much. There were times, especially when I was behind on assignments, where I leaned too heavily on AI to finish 
work quickly and sometimes submitted things without really understanding what was required. Looking back, those were 
usually the assignments where I performed the worst, and that taught me that AI can help you, but it can also hurt your
learning if you misuse it.

Going forward, I plan to keep using AI often because it is already part of how we work now and will only be more embedded 
in the future. But I would use it more intentionally — as a guide, not a crutch. For me, the value is in using it to help 
think through ideas, debug, and learn faster, while still doing the work of understanding the material myself. That has 
probably been the biggest lesson for me this semester.
